**Senior Product Manager: Task**

**Next steps**

Congratulations on reaching the task stage for the Senior Product Manager role at Xelix.

This task is about how you think. We want to see how you handle an ambiguous problem with commercial pressure behind it, and how you work now that AI is part of the job. What you recommend is secondary to how you get there.

Spend no more than three hours. If you run out of time, tell us what you cut and why. That is a useful answer.

---

**The brief**

**Keelwater** makes software that insurance companies use to handle claims. It has around 180 enterprise customers.

This is all you need to know about claims. When something goes wrong, say a car is damaged, the owner asks their insurer to pay. That request is a claim. A person at the insurer, called a handler, looks at each new claim and puts it in one of three piles:

* **Simple:** pay it quickly.

* **Needs a closer look:** check the details first.

* **Suspicious:** send it for investigation.

We call this sorting. Handlers spend most of their day in Keelwater. Any change to how sorting works changes their day, and changes how fast people get paid.

Over the last few months, more customers have asked for sorting to happen automatically.

* Several large customers have asked for it directly.

* Sales says it is blocking deals. Six open deals mention it, worth £2.3M a year between them. One is the biggest deal in the pipeline.

* Customer Success says it is hurting important accounts. Three have raised it in review meetings. One is a top-ten customer whose contract is up for renewal in five months.

* Around 40 support tickets in the last six months mention sorting.

* A competitor launched automatic sorting last quarter.

The product data is mixed.

* Keelwater customers sorted 2.1M claims in the last twelve months.

* Most are sorted quickly. The median time from a claim arriving to it being sorted is 11 minutes.

* Some take much longer. 15% take more than four hours.

* 3.4% of claims are moved to a different pile within 30 days.

A few more things you should know.

* Insurance companies must be able to explain every decision about a claim, to the customer and to their regulator.

* Keelwater's data team built an early prototype that predicts which pile a claim belongs in. It was put on hold 18 months ago when the team moved to other work.

* Your squad is halfway through building bulk sorting, which lets a handler sort many claims at once. It is due to ship in six weeks.

You have just taken ownership of this area. Your squad is a designer, a tech lead and five engineers.

---

**The task**

**Show us how you would move this forward.**

We are deliberately not prescribing a format. Decide what the situation needs and produce that. Whatever you produce, we want to understand:

1. What problem you think needs solving, and how confident you are.

2. What you would need to find out before committing the squad, and how you would find it out quickly.

3. How you would prioritise: what you would do first, what can wait and what you would not do. That includes the work the squad is already doing.

4. Take us from the first signal to a decision, then through validation, delivery and measurement. Show where AI changes how you work, where it does not, and what stays your responsibility. Before anything is automated, how would you decide it is safe and valuable enough to use?

5. What you would tell Sales and Customer Success, and when.

We ask for two more things at the end of your recording. Keep them to five minutes combined.

1. **How you used AI.** What you used it for, where you overrode it, and one thing it got wrong or led you towards that you rejected.

2. **How the lifecycle is changing.** Based on how you approached this task, how do you think the product development lifecycle is changing as teams become AI native? What would you prioritise in changing how you work to make Keelwater successful?

---

**Guidelines**

* Invent whatever you need: data, customer quotes, organisational detail. Flag what you invented.

* Use AI however you normally would. We use it heavily ourselves. We will ask about your process, so be ready to walk us through it.

* Assume a supportive but busy leadership team, and a commercial team that wants an answer.

* Keelwater is fictional. We will not use your work. This is an assessment, not a brief.

**Submitting**

1. Record a walkthrough of your work. **No more than 20 minutes.**

2. Send us the recording link, along with whatever you produced. Include one or two pieces of working material from the task: an AI conversation, an analysis, a prototype, an evaluation or an approach you dropped. We care more about what you learned and changed than how polished it is.
